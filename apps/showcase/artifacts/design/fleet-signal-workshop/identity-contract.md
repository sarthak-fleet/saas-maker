## Search frame

Fleet is a private operating practice and a public portfolio of independently
owned products. The identity must help the owner recognize coordination,
evidence, and momentum across products without making the products look like
one suite. It must work in quiet operator surfaces and expressive public or
editorial surfaces, remain legible from phone to desktop, and avoid invented
status, decorative agent activity, or a generic AI-startup shell.

## Identity thesis

**Independent products. Shared signals.**

Fleet is the visible coordination layer between products, not another product
that absorbs them. Its visual world is a **Signal Workshop**: sunlit limestone,
matte black structural frames, clear and seeded panes, three saturated signal
fields, and sparse route marks that make handoffs and evidence visible.

The formation mark contains three unequal signal panes moving through one open
frame. It represents different products sharing direction without becoming
identical. It must never become a literal boat, nautical badge, mascot, or
letter-in-a-circle software logo.

## References

### IBM 2x Grid

- Source: <https://www.ibm.com/design/language/2x-grid/>, accessed 2026-08-21.
- Lesson: use a small proportional chassis across UI, editorial material, and
  physical-looking compositions; combine repetition with controlled variation.
- Avoid: IBM typography, blue dominance, illustration assets, iconography, or
  enterprise-product styling.
- Fit: Fleet needs connective tissue across many media without forcing every
  execution to contain the same elements.

### NASA Graphics Standards

- Source: <https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/>, accessed
  2026-08-21.
- Lesson: a compact identifier, typography discipline, and supporting graphic
  rules can coordinate a wide program while mission imagery remains distinct.
- Avoid: NASA identifiers, the worm, insignia geometry, vehicle markings, and
  aerospace styling. NASA explicitly restricts those assets.
- Fit: Fleet also needs a small fixed layer around many independently legible
  programs and products.

### Schibsted Grotesk

- Source: <https://github.com/schibsted/schibsted-grotesk>, accessed
  2026-08-21.
- Lesson: one digital-first grotesque can carry interface text and large
  editorial display moments without a second decorative face.
- License evidence: SIL Open Font License 1.1 in the upstream repository.
- Fit: it is already used by SaaS Maker and preserves continuity with the
  strongest existing public Fleet surface.

## System

### Color

| Role | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limestone | `#EEE8DC` | Large daylight ground and quiet reading fields |
| Steel | `#11120F` | Structure, primary type, rules, and the formation mark |
| Cobalt | `#1746A2` | Active direction, canonical path, or primary public pane |
| Amber | `#C9820D` | Work in motion, review, or pending owner attention |
| Oxblood | `#7B211C` | Boundaries, blocked states, or consequential evidence |

Color owns a pane or state; it is not confetti. Never communicate state by hue
alone. Product colors remain inside product-owned surfaces.

### Typography

- Use Schibsted Grotesk for Fleet public, editorial, and shared portfolio
  surfaces.
- Use the native system sans for functional text inside Apple or platform-native
  products; Fleet identity must not weaken native readability.
- Use the native monospaced stack only for receipts, revisions, identifiers,
  and command evidence.
- Use weight, scale, and placement for hierarchy. Do not introduce a display
  serif or decorative mono.

### Composition

- Build on an 8-unit rhythm and divisions of two, then break symmetry with one
  large field and two or three smaller signal fields.
- Let black frames reveal ownership and boundaries. Do not put every object in
  a rounded card.
- Preserve generous negative space and one unmistakable focal relationship.
- Use route marks only when they explain a real handoff, lineage, dependency,
  or decision.

### Image and illustration language

- Use real product imagery when proof exists; frame it without recoloring the
  product into Fleet.
- For conceptual editorial work, use sparse black linework, clean grounds, and
  restrained structural color.
- Do not adopt Xiaohei as Fleet's mascot. The licensed Ian Xiaohei skill may be
  used for attributed editorial illustrations, but its character remains Ian's
  visual language rather than Fleet's identity.
- Avoid photorealistic AI people, abstract gradient clouds, glowing agent
  nodes, and decorative dashboards.

### Motion

- Use one authored signal movement: panes align, a route completes, or evidence
  passes through a frame.
- Hover and focus may backlight a whole pane; do not scatter floating,
  spring-loaded micro-motion.
- Reduced-motion surfaces show the resolved formation immediately.

## Representative contexts

1. **Public portfolio:** independent product names occupy differently sized
   clear or saturated panes inside one steel wall. Fleet supplies framing and
   wayfinding; each product supplies its own facts and destination.
2. **Operator ledger:** deploy, review, learn, and release appear as quiet,
   attributable rows. Route marks show actual progression or evidence rather
   than decorative activity.

## Anti-reference

Reject the generic Linear-clone or AI-operations dashboard: near-black canvas,
purple glow, rounded cards, tiny charts, agent avatars, and invented activity.
Also reject literal naval branding and a universal mascot. Both collapse
Fleet's real distinction: independently owned products coordinated through
visible evidence.

## Rollout boundary

Apply this identity first to Fleet-owned meta-surfaces: the SaaS Maker public
directory, portfolio strip, operator receipts, shared campaign headers, and
Fleet editorial artifacts. Do not reskin independent products. Each product's
`PRODUCT.md` and `DESIGN.md` remain authoritative.
