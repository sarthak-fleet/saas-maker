# Style DNA

## Core qualities

Pure white, minimal, hand-drawn, spacious, restrained, absurd, and structurally
clear without becoming instructional. It should resemble a quick explanatory
sketch made by a product builder, not a finished marketing graphic.

## Required traits

- Use a 16:9 horizontal canvas.
- Use a pure white background without warm gray, paper texture, gradients,
  shadows, noise, or vintage effects.
- Draw mainly with thin black lines that wobble slightly and do not look
  mechanical, heavy, or vector-perfect.
- Keep the subject to roughly 40%-60% of the canvas and preserve at least 35%
  quiet white space.
- Use no more than 5-8 brief handwritten English annotations, ideally 1-4 words
  each.
- Explain one action, structure, state, or metaphor per image.
- Let the scene express the structure; never print the composition type on the
  image.

## Color roles

- **Black:** line art, Xiaohei, objects, structure, and primary labels.
- **Orange:** the main path, flow, arrow, or movement from one state to another.
- **Red:** the one important warning, problem, emotion, or result.
- **Blue:** secondary notes, feedback, system state, or assistant/automation
  context.

Blue is optional. Use less color when in doubt.

## Exclusions

Do not make commercial key art, a slide infographic, a formal flowchart, a
course handout, a children's illustration, polished flat vector art, a complex
architecture diagram, a technology UI, or a realistic app screenshot. Avoid
busy backgrounds, rigid grids, exhaustive node labels, and type labels such as
"Workflow", "Architecture", "Common mistakes", or "Roadmap" in the corner.

Aim for strange, inventive, clean, and immediately legible. Avoid cute,
childish, ornate, or stiff.
